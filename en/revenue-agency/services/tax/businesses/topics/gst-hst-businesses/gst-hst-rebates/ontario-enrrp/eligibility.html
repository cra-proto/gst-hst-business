Ontario enhanced new residential rental property (ENRRP) rebate

Who can apply

Learn about the eligibility criteria and restrictions for the Ontario ENRRP rebate.

On this page

  • Eligibility criteria
  • Restrictions

Eligibility criteria

Check the following criteria to see if you may be eligible for this rebate.

If you lease both the building and land

You may be eligible if all the following apply:

  • You are eligible for the Ontario NRRP rebate
  • Construction or substantial renovation is substantially completed by December 31, 2029
  • Tax becomes payable by December 31, 2032
  • The fair market value (FMV) of the housing is less than $1.85 million
  • You leased the housing or residential units in the housing to another person for long-term residential use by an individual

You must also meet the following criteria depending on whether you are a purchaser or a builder:

If you are a purchaser/landlord

You are a purchaser/landlord if you paid the GST/HST on the purchase of newly constructed or substantially renovated housing, or on the purchase of an interest in the housing.

You maybe be eligible if construction or substantial renovation began:

  • Between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027

  • Some time before March 31, 2026, but you entered into an agreement of purchase and sale for a single unit residential complex or residential condominium unit between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027

If you are a builder/landlord

You are a builder/landlord if you reported and remitted the GST/HST on a self-supply of newly constructed or substantially renovated housing or an addition to a multiple unit residential complex.

You may be eligible if:

  • Construction or substantial renovation began between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027
If you sell the building and lease the land

You are a builder who reported and remitted the GST/HST on a self-supply of housing or an addition to a multiple-unit residential complex and made an exempt sale of the building together with an exempt long-term lease of the land under a single written agreement.

You may be eligible if all the following apply:

  • You are eligible for the Ontario NRRP rebate
  • The purchaser of the building is eligible for the GST/HST new housing rebate
  • Construction or substantial renovation began between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027
  • Construction or substantial renovation is substantially completed by December 31, 2029
  • Tax becomes payable by December 31, 2032
  • The fair market value (FMV) of the housing is less than $1.85 million
If you are a co-operative housing corporation (co-op)

You may be eligible if all the following apply:

  • You are eligible for the Ontario NRRP rebate
  • Construction or substantial renovation is substantially completed by December 31, 2029
  • Tax becomes payable by December 31, 2032
  • The fair market value (FMV) of the housing is less than $1.85 million
  • You leased a residential unit for long-term residential use by an individual

You must also meet the following criteria depending on whether you are a purchaser or a builder:

If you are a co-op purchaser/landlord

You are a co-op purchaser/landlord if you are a co-operative housing corporation that paid GST/HST on the purchase of newly constructed or substantially renovated housing, or on the purchase of an interest in the housing from a builder.

You maybe be eligible if construction or substantial renovation began:

  • Between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027

  • Some time before March 31, 2026, but you entered into an agreement of purchase and sale for a single unit residential complex or residential condominium unit between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027

If you are a co-op builder/landlord

You are a co-op builder/landlord if you are a co-operative housing corporation that reported and remitted the GST/HST on a self-supply of housing that is a residential complex or an addition to a multiple-unit residential complex.

You may be eligible if:

  • Construction or substantial renovation began between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027
If you lease the land

You may be eligible if all the following apply:

  • You are eligible for the Ontario NRRP rebate
  • You reported and remitted the GST/HST on a self-supply at the time of the first lease or the time of a change in use of the land and the supply is either:
    • A lease of land, other than a site in a residential trailer park, which provides continuous possession for a person in occupation or possession of a residential unit affixed to that land and used as a place of residence for individuals
    • A lease of a site in a residential trailer park for a person in occupation or possession of a mobile home, travel trailer, motor home or similar vehicle or trailer
  • Tax is deemed to have been paid between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027
  • The fair market value (FMV) or basic tax content of the land is less than $610,500

Determine when construction begins

The CRA considers construction of a residential complex to have begun when excavation work relating to the complex began.

Activities that occur before excavation, such as signing a purchase and sale agreement, demolition, site or environmental testing, plan development and finalization, clearing, remediation or reclamation activities, obtaining permits, contracting for excavation, or road construction, do not determine when construction begins.

Example of determining when construction began

A builder/landlord plans to construct a multiple unit residential complex that will be leased to individuals for long-term residential use. Before excavation begins, the builder/landlord conducts site and environmental testing, develops building plans, obtains a building permit, and enters into a contract for excavation. Although these activities occur before excavation, construction does not begin until the excavation work relating to the residential complex actually starts.

If excavation work relating to the residential complex begins on July 15, 2026, the CRA considers construction of the residential complex to have begun on that date. As July 15, 2026, falls between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027, the construction start requirement for Ontario ENRRP rebate may be met, provided all other eligibility conditions are satisfied.

Determine when tax becomes payable

When tax becomes payable depends on your situation.

Find the details that apply to you under the appropriate application type on How to apply.

Restrictions

The following restrictions apply where eligibility for the Ontario ENRRP rebate is based, in part, on an agreement of purchase and sale entered into between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027.

Assignment sales
For assignment sales, to be eligible for the Ontario ENRRP rebate, both the initial agreement of purchase and sale with the builder and the assignment agreement must be entered into between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027.
Change in agreement
If you entered into an agreement with a builder before April 1, 2026, and you make changes to the agreement such that it is considered to be entered into on or after April 1, 2026, for the purposes of eligibility for the Ontario ENRRP rebate, the agreement is deemed to have been entered into before April 1, 2026, and you are not entitled to the rebate.
New agreement
If you entered into an agreement with a builder before April 1, 2026, the agreement is terminated and another agreement is entered into on or after April 1, 2026, to be eligible for the Ontario ENRRP rebate, the new agreement must have been entered into for bona fide purposes other than to obtain the Ontario ENRRP rebate.
Interaction with the purpose-built rental housing rebate
A landlord cannot claim both the Ontario ENRRP rebate and the purpose-built rental housing rebate (PBRH rebate) for the same housing.
If housing qualifies for the PBRH rebate, the Ontario ENRRP rebate is not available for that housing.
For more information on the PBRH rebate, refer to GST/HST Memorandum 19-3-9, Purpose-built Rental Housing Rebate.

Fair market value (FMV)

Fair market value generally means the highest price, expressed in dollars, that property or services would bring in an open and unrestricted market, between a willing buyer and a willing seller who are both knowledgeable, informed, and prudent, and who are acting independently of each other. Fair market value does not include the GST/HST payable on the fair market value of the property. For sales of real property, fair market value does not include any provincial land transfer taxes payable on the sale.

Single unit residential complex

Single unit residential complex means a residential complex that does not contain more than one residential unit, but does not include a residential condominium unit. For the purposes of the GST/HST new housing rebate and the GST/HST new residential rental property rebate, reference to a single unit residential complex includes a residential complex containing not more than two residential units (for example, a duplex).

A single unit residential complex generally includes other structures near or adjacent to the unit, such as detached garages or sheds. It also includes the land subjacent and immediately contiguous to the unit that can reasonably be regarded as contributing to the use and enjoyment of the unit as a place of residence. The CRA considers that up to half a hectare (1.23 acres) of land may be reasonably necessary for the use and enjoyment of a unit. However, in some cases, more than half a hectare of land may be considered to form part of the complex.

Substantial renovation (for the purposes of new housing rebates)

Substantial renovation (for the purposes of new housing rebates) – Major changes have to be made to meet the definition of a substantial renovation. In a major renovation project, the interior of a building is essentially gutted. Generally, 90% or more of the interior of the existing housing has to be removed or replaced to qualify as a substantial renovation (the 90% test).

You do not have to remove or replace the foundation, exterior and interior supporting walls, roof, floors, and staircases to meet the 90% test.

Only livable areas count towards a substantial renovation, including finished basements and finished attics. Livable areas do not include garages or crawl spaces. Work done to partially complete a basement but not make it a livable basement does not count toward the 90% test.

If you converted a non-residential property into a house or constructed a major addition onto an existing house and, along with that addition, you also substantially renovated the existing house, the property would generally meet the definition of a substantial renovation for the purposes of this rebate.

Substantial completion

Substantial completion generally means that the construction or substantial renovation of a house is at a stage of completion (generally 90% or more) where an individual can reasonably inhabit the premises. Minor repairs, adjustments, or outstanding upgrades are not considered to impair the use and enjoyment of the house as a place of residence.

Residential units

Residential unit means:

  • A detached house, semi-detached house, row house unit, condominium unit, mobile home, floating home, or apartment
  • A suite or room in a hotel, a motel, an inn, a boarding house or a lodging house, or in a residence for students, seniors, individuals with a disability, or other individuals
  • Any other similar premises or the part of such housing listed above that:
    • Is occupied by an individual as a place of residence or lodging
    • Is supplied by way of lease, licence or similar arrangement as a place of residence or lodging for individuals
    • Is vacant, but was last occupied or supplied as a place of residence or lodging for individuals
    • Has never been used or occupied for any purpose, but is intended to be used as a place of residence or lodging for individuals

Residential condominium unit

Residential condominium unit means a residential unit that is for use as a place of residence and that is, or is intended to be, a bounded space in a building designated or described as a separate unit on a registered condominium or strata lot plan or description (or similar plan or description registered under provincial law). A residential condominium unit includes any interest in the common areas as well as any interest in land pertaining to the ownership of the unit.

For example, an apartment unit or a townhouse would generally be a residential condominium unit if the unit:

  • Is a residential complex that is for use as a place of residence
  • Is registered as a condominium unit under provincial law
  • Is owned under a deeded title separate from any other unit in the condominium building
  • Can be sold separately from the other units in the condominium building

Multiple unit residential complex

Multiple unit residential complex means a residential complex that contains more than one residential unit, but does not include a condominium complex. For the purposes of the GST/HST new residential rental property rebate, a multiple unit residential complex does not include a duplex.

Addition to a multiple unit residential complex

Addition to a multiple unit residential complex is generally considered to be construction that expands the building envelope of an existing multiple unit residential complex.
