Ontario enhanced new residential rental property (ENRRP) rebate

What is the ENRRP rebate

The Ontario ENRRP rebate is a temporary rebate that, together with the Ontario new residential rental property (NRRP) rebate, provides landlords a combined rebate of up to 100% of the 8% provincial part of the HST on a newly constructed, substantially renovated, or converted home.

This rebate is generally available between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027, depending on additional eligibility criteria.

The Ontario ENRRP rebate generally applies to housing or land that will be leased as a place of residence for individuals in the following situations:

  • The purchase of new or substantially renovated housing, or the construction, substantial renovation, or addition of housing
  • The sale of new residential housing on leased land, or the assignment of a leasehold interest in the land on which the housing is situated
  • The purchase of new or substantially renovated housing, or the construction, substantial renovation, or addition of housing, by a co-operative housing corporation
  • The lease of land for residential purposes

How much the rebate is

The Ontario ENRRP rebate, combined with the Ontario NRRP rebate, provides:

Combined rebate amount
New home value Rebate amount
Up to $1 million Full rebate (100% of the 8% provincial part of the HST paid, up to $80,000)
 Above $1 million, up to $1.5 million  Flat rebate of $80,000
Above $1.5 million and below $1.85 million Partial rebate of the provincial part of HST paid 
$1.85 million and above   Only the Ontario new housing rebate of up to $24,000 is available
Rebate amounts for leases of land

Different rebate thresholds apply to leases of land for residential purposes. For these leases, the maximum combined rebate is $26,400 and is gradually reduced when the fair market value of the land is $495,000 or more.

Ontario new home affordability payment (ONHAP)

A landlord who is eligible for the Ontario ENRRP rebate may also be eligible for the Ontario new home affordability payment (ONHAP), administered by the Province of Ontario. The ONHAP provides an amount of up to 100% of the federal part of the HST paid on a new or substantially renovated home, to a maximum of $50,000, for homes valued up to $1.85 million.

The ONHAP is reduced by any GST/HST new residential rental property rebate of the federal part of the HST to which the landlord is entitled.

A separate rebate application is not required to claim the ONHAP. Fill out the “Consent to share information” section on page 2 of Form GST524 to allow the CRA to provide the Province of Ontario with the relevant information (i.e., contact, banking and rebate information) to administer this payment.

Substantial renovation (for the purposes of new housing rebates)

Substantial renovation (for the purposes of new housing rebates) – Major changes have to be made to meet the definition of a substantial renovation. In a major renovation project, the interior of a building is essentially gutted. Generally, 90% or more of the interior of the existing housing has to be removed or replaced to qualify as a substantial renovation (the 90% test).

You do not have to remove or replace the foundation, exterior and interior supporting walls, roof, floors, and staircases to meet the 90% test.

Only livable areas count towards a substantial renovation, including finished basements and finished attics. Livable areas do not include garages or crawl spaces. Work done to partially complete a basement but not make it a livable basement does not count toward the 90% test.

If you converted a non-residential property into a house or constructed a major addition onto an existing house and, along with that addition, you also substantially renovated the existing house, the property would generally meet the definition of a substantial renovation for the purposes of this rebate.

Primary place of residence

Primary place of residence of an individual generally means the residence that the individual lives in on a permanent basis. An individual may have more than one residence, but is considered to have only one primary place of residence. For more information, see GST/HST Policy Statement P-228, Primary Place of Residence.
