Ontario enhanced new residential rental property (ENRRP) rebate

  • What is the ENRRP rebate
  • Who can apply
  • How to apply
  • After you apply

What is the ENRRP rebate

The Ontario ENRRP rebate is a temporary rebate that, together with the Ontario new residential rental property (NRRP) rebate, provides landlords a combined rebate of up to 100% of the 8% provincial part of the HST on a newly constructed, substantially renovated, or converted home.

The Ontario ENRRP rebate is for housing or land that will be leased as a place of residence for individuals. Generally, it is available between April 1, 2026, and March 31, 2027, and applies to the following situations:

  • The purchase of new or substantially renovated housing, or the construction, substantial renovation, or addition of housing
  • The sale of new residential housing on leased land, or the assignment of a leasehold interest in the land on which the housing is situated
  • The purchase of new or substantially renovated housing, or the construction, substantial renovation, or addition of housing, by a co-operative housing corporation
  • The lease of land for residential purposes

How much the rebate is

The amount of the rebate depends on the fair market value (FMV) of the home or land.

Rebate amounts for a home

The Ontario ENRRP rebate, combined with the Ontario NRRP rebate, provides the following amounts for:

  • The lease of building and land
  • The sale of new housing on leased land or an assignment of a leasehold interest in the land on which the building is situated
  • A co-operative housing corporation that leases a residential unit and land
Combined rebate amount for a home
FMV of the home Combined rebate amount
Up to $1 million Full rebate (up to 100% of the 8% provincial part of the HST, up to $80,000)
 Between $1 million and $1.5 million  Flat rebate of $80,000
Between $1.5 million and $1.85 million Ontario ENRRP rebate is gradually reduced as the FMV increases 
$1.85 million or more   Only the Ontario NRRP rebate of up to $24,000 is available

Interactions with other rebates

When you are eligible for more than one rebate, they will interact together (if applicable) in the following ways:

  • The Ontario NRRP rebate is reduced by the Ontario new housing rebate
  • The Ontario ENRRP rebate is reduced by whichever is bigger:

    • Ontario first-time home buyers' rebate

    • Ontario enhanced new housing rebate

Examples of rebate amounts for a home
Example rebate: Lease of building and land

An eligible landlord purchases a new single unit residential complex residential complex for $1.25 million.

The landlord is entitled to the maximum combined Ontario NRRP and Ontario ENRRP rebate of $80,000, as no reduction applies until the FMV exceeds $1.5 million.

The landlord is eligible for the Ontario NRRP rebate of $24,000, and the additional Ontario ENRRP rebate of $56,000, for a combined rebate amount of $80,000.

Example rebate: Sale of building and lease of land

An eligible landlord (other than a co-op) purchases a single-unit new home for $1.25 million, sells the building for $864,000 and leases the land under a long-term lease to the purchaser of the building. The purchaser is eligible for the Ontario new housing rebate ($24,000), the Ontario first-time home buyers’ rebate ($24,178) and the Ontario enhanced new housing rebate ($37,171).

The maximum combined Ontario NRRP and Ontario ENRRP rebates available to the landlord before any reductions is $80,000.

The maximum $24,000 Ontario NRRP rebate available to the landlord is reduced to zero as the purchaser’s new housing rebate is also $24,000. The additional Ontario ENRRP rebate of $56,000 ($80,000 − $24,000) is then reduced by $37,171, which is the greater of the Ontario first-time homebuyers’ rebate and the Ontario enhanced new housing rebate available to the purchaser of the building.

As a result, the landlord is eligible for a combined Ontario NRRP ($0) and Ontario ENRRP rebate of $18,829 ($56,000 minus $37,171).

Example rebate: Co-operative housing corporation

An eligible co-op landlord purchases a new single unit residential complex for $1.25 million and sells a share of the capital stock of the co-op to an individual for $864,000. The purchaser of the share is eligible for the Ontario new housing rebate ($24,000), the Ontario first-time homebuyers’ rebate ($37,171) and the Ontario enhanced new housing rebate ($37,171).

The maximum combined Ontario NRRP and Ontario ENRRP rebates available to the co-op before any reductions is $80,000.

The maximum $24,000 Ontario NRRP rebate available to the co-op is reduced to zero as the purchaser’s new housing rebate is also $24,000. The additional Ontario ENRRP rebate of $56,000 ($80,000 minus $24,000) is then reduced by $37,171, which is the greater of the Ontario first-time homebuyers’ rebate and the Ontario enhanced new housing rebate available to the purchaser of the share.

As a result, the co-op is eligible for a combined Ontario NRRP ($0) and Ontario ENRRP rebate of $18,829 ($56,000 minus $37,171).

Rebate amounts for land

For an exempt supply of a lease of land for a period of continuous possession or use of at least one month that results in a deemed supply, the Ontario ENRRP rebate, combined with the Ontario NRRP rebate, generally provides the following:

Combined rebate amount for land
FMV or basic tax content of the land Combined rebate amount
Up to $330,000 Full rebate (up to 100% of the 8% provincial part of the HST, up to $26,400)
 Between $330,000 and $495,000  Flat rebate of $26,400
Between $495,000 and $610,500 Ontario ENRRP rebate is gradually reduced as the FMV or basic tax content increases 
$1.85 million or more   Only the Ontario NRRP rebate of up to $7,920 is available
Determine the FMV for a trailer park

For the lease of a site in a residential trailer park or on an addition to a residential trailer park, the FMV for each site is determined by dividing the FMV of the park (or the addition) by the number of sites in the park (or the addition).

Example of a rebate amount for land

An eligible landlord leased a site in a residential trailer park that resulted in the landlord being deemed to have made a self-supply at the time of the lease.

The FMV of the site is $400,000.

The landlord is entitled to the maximum combined Ontario NRRP and Ontario ENRRP rebate of $26,400, as no reduction applies until the FMV exceeds $495,000.

The landlord is eligible for the Ontario NRRP rebate of $7,920 and the additional Ontario ENRRP rebate of $18,480, for a combined rebate amount of $26,400.

Ontario new home affordability payment (ONHAP)

A landlord who is eligible for the Ontario ENRRP rebate may also be eligible for the Ontario new home affordability payment (ONHAP), administered by the Province of Ontario. The ONHAP provides an amount of up to 100% of the federal part of the HST paid on a new or substantially renovated home, to a maximum of $50,000, for homes valued up to $1.85 million.

The ONHAP is reduced by any GST/HST new residential rental property rebate of the federal part of the HST to which the landlord is entitled.

A separate rebate application is not required to claim the ONHAP. Fill out the “Consent to share information” section on page 2 of Form GST524 to allow the CRA to provide the Province of Ontario with the relevant information (i.e., contact, banking and rebate information) to administer this payment.

Fair market value (FMV)

Fair market value generally means the highest price, expressed in dollars, that property or services would bring in an open and unrestricted market, between a willing buyer and a willing seller who are both knowledgeable, informed, and prudent, and who are acting independently of each other. Fair market value does not include the GST/HST payable on the fair market value of the property. For sales of real property, fair market value does not include any provincial land transfer taxes payable on the sale.

Single unit residential complex

Single unit residential complex means a residential complex that does not contain more than one residential unit, but does not include a residential condominium unit. For the purposes of the GST/HST new housing rebate and the GST/HST new residential rental property rebate, reference to a single unit residential complex includes a residential complex containing not more than two residential units (for example, a duplex).

A single unit residential complex generally includes other structures near or adjacent to the unit, such as detached garages or sheds. It also includes the land subjacent and immediately contiguous to the unit that can reasonably be regarded as contributing to the use and enjoyment of the unit as a place of residence. The CRA considers that up to half a hectare (1.23 acres) of land may be reasonably necessary for the use and enjoyment of a unit. However, in some cases, more than half a hectare of land may be considered to form part of the complex.

Substantial renovation (for the purposes of new housing rebates)

Substantial renovation (for the purposes of new housing rebates) – Major changes have to be made to meet the definition of a substantial renovation. In a major renovation project, the interior of a building is essentially gutted. Generally, 90% or more of the interior of the existing housing has to be removed or replaced to qualify as a substantial renovation (the 90% test).

You do not have to remove or replace the foundation, exterior and interior supporting walls, roof, floors, and staircases to meet the 90% test.

Only livable areas count towards a substantial renovation, including finished basements and finished attics. Livable areas do not include garages or crawl spaces. Work done to partially complete a basement but not make it a livable basement does not count toward the 90% test.

If you converted a non-residential property into a house or constructed a major addition onto an existing house and, along with that addition, you also substantially renovated the existing house, the property would generally meet the definition of a substantial renovation for the purposes of this rebate.

Primary place of residence

Primary place of residence of an individual generally means the residence that the individual lives in on a permanent basis. An individual may have more than one residence, but is considered to have only one primary place of residence. For more information, see GST/HST Policy Statement P-228, Primary Place of Residence.
